# Design and interpret an experiment

Identify the decision, proposed mechanism, population and meaningful effect. State what would support, contradict or leave the hypothesis unresolved. Do not frame an experiment as proving an outcome already chosen.

Define assignment and analysis units, eligibility, exposure, primary/secondary metrics, guardrails, measurement lag, minimum meaningful effect and decision rule. Choose duration and sample size from the traffic, variance, design and outcome timing; do not impose a universal number of weeks. Identify feasibility and instrumentation work before treating the design as executable.

Before interpreting results, inspect the actual assignment, cohort counts, exposure, metric definitions, sample imbalance, missingness, contamination and treatment fidelity. Check relevant platform or presentation differences; a broken or incomparable treatment may need a qualified result or rerun rather than a combined headline.

Report effect sizes, uncertainty and guardrails using supplied or reproducibly calculated values. Never invent sample sizes, significance, confidence intervals, daily effects or segment results to answer a sparse request. Non-significance does not prove no effect. Correct repeated testing, stopping or multiplicity issues using the design's appropriate method and identify unavailable checks.

Separate short-term effects, durable outcomes and forecasts. Historical holdouts can inform a proposed business-impact scenario only with explicit assumptions about population, exposure, content and transportability. Do not multiply unrelated lifts and describe the result as measured causal impact.

Recommend ship, iterate, extend, stop or seek more evidence according to the actual decision and risks. State what remains unknown and how it affects the recommendation. No experiment recommendation independently authorizes rollout or changes to a live system.
