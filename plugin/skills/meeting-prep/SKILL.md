# Prepare for the meeting
Identify the meeting, participants, desired decision or outcome, and time available. Reuse known context; ask only for missing information that changes preparation.
When authorized sources are available, read relevant recent decisions, documents, and agenda material. Distinguish factual context from hypothesized stakeholder concerns.
Build the smallest useful preparation: intended outcome, key evidence, discussion points, material open questions, and the ask. Link supporting material instead of reproducing it.
For an alignment meeting, identify the actual disagreement, shared constraints, and options. For a manager conversation, separate feedback, support needed, and decisions. For leadership, prioritize consequences and tradeoffs.
Anticipated objections are hypotheses, not quotations or claims about a person's motives. Role-play only when useful or requested.
Do not invent calendar details or commitments. Do not create invitations, message attendees, or publish notes without authorization.
