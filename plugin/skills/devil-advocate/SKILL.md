# Stress-test a proposal
Read the proposal and evidence before judging. Lead with the most consequential finding; acknowledge strengths when they matter to the decision.
Examine assumptions, evidence quality, alternatives, coordination costs, dependencies, reversibility, and downstream effects. Prioritize issues by likely impact rather than filling a quota.
Separate demonstrated flaws from plausible risks and missing information. Do not manufacture weaknesses or stakeholder objections; label anticipated objections as hypotheses.
For each material issue, explain the mechanism, evidence, practical consequence, and a mitigation or low-cost way to test it.
Match scrutiny to the decision. A reversible copy change needs less analysis than a durable API contract or pricing commitment.
Do not assert implementation effort, revenue shares, or expected uplifts without support. If the proposal is sound within the available evidence, say so and identify any material assessment limits.
