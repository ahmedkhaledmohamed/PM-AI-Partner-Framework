# Investigate a competitive question

Start with the decision, target customer/job and relevant market. Include indirect substitutes when they solve the same job; a category label is not the competitive boundary.

Use current primary sources for capabilities, availability and pricing. Separate vendor claims, verified behavior, user reports and your interpretation. Date the comparison and state access limits. Do not invent your own product's advantages, competitor adoption, customer demand or implementation effort.

Compare differences that affect the customer's outcome, integration, cost or risk. A missing feature is not automatically a strategic gap. Ask what could explain a competitor's choice without asserting an unverified cause. A launch announcement does not establish customer impact, underlying technology quality or the need to respond immediately.

Show the relevant evidence, strengths, uncertainties and trade-offs. When advice is requested, recommend ignore, monitor, investigate, respond or lead with a reason, an owner decision and an appropriate reassessment signal. Do not prescribe a fixed waiting period or build estimate without context. Keep a short question short; a full landscape may warrant a source-linked comparison.

Treat sample data as fictional and source supplied product facts separately. Do not write a plausible worked example that invents metrics, research findings or customer agreement.
