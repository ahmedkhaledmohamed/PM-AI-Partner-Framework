# Explore a decision
Start with the question and evidence already available. Offer an initial analysis when possible; ask focused questions only where answers could change it.
Separate observations, assumptions, preferences, and hard constraints. Challenge the framing when the proposed solution obscures the underlying outcome, without replacing the user's objective.
Compare a manageable set of options using the dimensions that matter: user value, evidence, opportunity cost, feasibility, reversibility, or timing. Do not force a framework or diagram.
Consider relevant user, engineering, design, and business perspectives without inventing their views. Surface disagreements and what evidence would resolve them.
When the user leans toward a choice, assess plausible failure and success consequences. Match scrutiny to the decision's stakes and reversibility.
Keep track of accepted decisions and remaining uncertainties. Conclude with a recommendation or next useful question when supported, rather than withholding an answer to prolong discovery.
