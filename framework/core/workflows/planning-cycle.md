# Plan a cycle from requests and evidence

Use for reviewing unfinished work, new requests, priorities and capacity. Start from the target organization's canonical planning guidance and actual source systems rather than importing another cycle's terminology.

## Establish the review set

Retrieve the declared periods, ownership scope, statuses and source types with complete pagination and dates. Preserve original requests and manual decisions. Reconcile open delivery records with completed, routine-support, transferred and dropped work; do not equate tracker cancellation with a cancelled capability.

Review unfinished work first when it affects capacity. Decisions may include finish this cycle, split and continue, split and drop, carry, or drop; use the team's agreed vocabulary. Separate confirmed continuation from possible rollover. Keep selected scope in its maintained home and supporting review inputs linked to it.

## Define the proposed change

For each independently reviewable job/deliverable, show current support, requested change, evidence, dependencies and the decision needed. One request may yield several rows; one underlying implementation may contribute to multiple completion criteria. Keep one estimate for shared work and readable titles beside IDs.

Separate enduring workstreams and success measures from cycle-specific sponsorship and priority. Link a genuine delivery home when scope matches; do not force an initiative or completion criterion just to fill a cell. Routine support may be a valid contribution, and collaboration may require discovery before a build commitment.

## Decide and investigate

Present concrete proposed scope rather than an empty request for approval. Record the accepted decision, accountable person and provenance. Where required, review cross-team alignment before assigning investigation owners. Engineering can then check existing behavior, technical changes, dependencies, rollout and rough effort. Missing evidence or ownership remains explicit.

## Maintain the result

The planning record owns selected scope; delivery systems own current tracking state. Workstream and leadership views derive from the same records. Preserve reviews and estimates, re-read record identities after user sorting, and verify changed cells, validations, colors and links after a write. Do not silently recreate discarded proposals or duplicate work across contribution homes.

Stop the agreed planning stage with a reviewable scope and open decisions. Further delivery discovery, prioritization or commitment is another explicitly scoped step.
