# Evaluate usefulness with three PMs

Status: protocol prepared; no participants recruited, sessions observed or results recorded. The owner arranges participation. This document does not authorize contacting anyone or publishing participant work.

## Question and investment decision

Can a PM bring a real product question, identify relevant evidence and produce a reviewable decision brief using this guide and their existing agent? Do they choose to use the method again without a reminder?

Recruit three PMs beyond the author who have a current question and authorized access to the sources needed to investigate it. Include differing levels of agent experience where practical, and record the mix. This is a small qualitative pilot, not evidence of population-wide adoption, productivity gains or causal impact.

Pause feature expansion during the pilot. Afterward, decide whether to develop the starter tooling, keep a concise guide and examples, or stop active development. Software checks and favorable comments alone do not answer the usefulness question.

## First task

1. Before showing the guide, ask how the participant would normally approach their question. Record the intended reader, decision and completion condition, existing setup and expected difficulties. Do not request confidential task content.
2. Give them the README, operating model and fictional worked example. Let them use their existing workspace; initialization is optional. Record the source revision, runtime and selected skills so observations can be interpreted.
3. Allow up to 60 minutes for an observed attempt. Record time spent on setup and the task separately, where they stop or ask for help, corrections they make and each intervention by the observer. Record unfinished attempts too. An assisted result is not independent completion.
4. Review the brief against the criteria below. Ask what changed their thinking, what was unnecessary, what remains wrong and whether the output is ready for its intended reader. Record their assessment separately from the observer's.

The participant controls the product decision and source access. Missing evidence should remain explicit. Do not introduce connectors, build tools or repair accounts to rescue a session; record the dependency and any resulting limitation.

## Brief review

For each criterion, record met, partial, unmet or unassessable, with a concrete observation. Do not reduce the review to one score.

| Criterion | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | The question, reader and proposed next action are explicit |
| Source authority | Requests, accepted intent, inspected behavior and measured outcomes are distinguished where relevant |
| Traceability | Material claims link to inspected sources with relevant scope or dates; unsupported claims are qualified |
| Uncertainty | Missing or conflicting evidence and its implications are visible |
| Human review | The brief identifies needed decisions without inventing agreement, ownership or delivery commitments |

Ask the participant to correct the brief before sharing it. Record material corrections and remaining defects. If an authorized reviewer cannot inspect a source, mark the relevant criterion unassessable rather than assuming correctness. Only claim usefulness for the task stage actually observed.

## Repeat use

Agree on a 14-day observation window after the first attempt. Do not send usage reminders. At the end, ask whether another suitable task arose and whether they used the method again before that follow-up. Record voluntary reuse, deliberate non-use, no opportunity or unknown separately. Record whether reuse was observed or only self-reported, what they reused and whether they needed help. A stated intention to return is not repeat use.

## Private observation record

Keep one record per participant in the owner's authorized private research location. Use participant codes. Record task category, experience, source revision, assistance, setup/task times, review findings, corrections and repeat-use evidence. Do not put participant identity, raw conversations, employer documents, source links or original briefs in this public repository. Publish only a separately reviewed aggregate summary that participants have permitted sharing.

No results are available yet. Do not populate example results or count the fictional walkthrough as a participant session.

## Review after the pilot

Compare all three attempts, including failures and assistance. Identify which improvements came from the method, which required the author's coaching, and where attribution remains uncertain. Keep timing observations descriptive; tasks and experience differ and there is no controlled comparison.

- Continue focused tooling only if at least two PMs complete a brief without substantive author intervention, all applicable criteria are met, and at least two independently choose to reuse the method on another suitable task. Inspect the actual friction before choosing any feature. This is a working investment rule, not a validated performance threshold.
- If the method helps but users prefer their existing tools, maintain the guide and worked examples. Do not infer demand for an installer from demand for the explanation.
- If neither useful completion nor repeat use is supported, stop feature investment. Keep a concise reference or archive active development after the owner's review.
- If there are too few suitable tasks, inaccessible evidence or missing follow-up, report the decision as unresolved. Do not turn missing observations into successes or failures; the owner decides whether another bounded round is worthwhile.

Any release, external communication or change to an existing proposal still follows its own approval requirements.
